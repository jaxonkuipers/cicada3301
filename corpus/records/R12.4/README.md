# R12.4 — timed domain, physical carriers and primary enrollment

At the appointed time, `845145127.com` displayed “Find our symbol at the location nearest you” and fourteen [coordinates](coordinates.csv). Reapplying OutGuess to `cicada.jpg` exposed a [signed subset of twelve](artifacts/communications/2012-01-coordinates.asc), authenticating the physical-posting instruction. Posters recovered in Warsaw, Paris, Miami, Arkansas and Sydney carried QR codes to numbered JPEGs on the domain.

OutGuess on those carriers produced two signed 22-position book codes. The poem clue [“A poem of fading death, named for a king”](artifacts/communications/2012-01-book-code-poem.asc) selected William Gibson's *Agrippa* and resolved exactly to `sq6wmgv2zcsrix6t.onion`. The [“twenty-nine volumes” clue](artifacts/communications/2012-01-twenty-nine-volumes-book-code.asc) is commonly associated with the Britannica `CICADA` article; the preserved source resolves 18 of 22 positions, leaving its exact edition open.

`sq6wmgv2zcsrix6t.onion/cgi-bin/get_email` displayed a [signed enrollment form](artifacts/communications/2012-01-anonymous-email-enrollment.asc). It directed the solver to create a fresh public-webmail address through Tor, submit it, wait for a number and revisit the onion at `/<number>`; the example was `3894894230934209`. That private path passes to [R12.5](../R12.5/).

Ten carrier identifiers later reappeared in R12.6's signed whitespace release. The twenty-nine-volumes family comprised `162667212858`, `316744223127`, `414974253863`, `427566844663`, `598852142735` and `889296759263`; the *Agrippa* family comprised `644169769482`, `876873892385`, `935691396441` and `963846244281`. Several physical carriers, the exact Britannica source, four readout characters, server bytes and enrollment state remain unavailable.
