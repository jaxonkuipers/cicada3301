[7/15/2021 5:23 PM] mrgermanpiano (pinned)
Another response that I received:

I've heard of 3301 and the liber primus before.
The illustration on page 27 is certainly based on a cicada wing, though it's obviously hand-drawn and not traced, because it has a few minor details that are anomalous, the kind of thing that would happen if a person were trying to reproduce a wing, drawing by eye, and getting some of the proportions messed up. The bottom line is that there are no cicadas whose wing venation exactly matches that drawing, though some of the more well-known genera (Neotibicen, Psaltoda) have reasonably similar venation, allowing for some "artistic license". There are no other insects whose basic venation pattern would correspond. There are over 3,000 species of cicadas, nearly all in a single family.
My suspicion is that it's got nothing necessarily to do with the cicada itself, or even cicadas in general, and more likely to do with the recent research on the bactericidal and hydrophobic properties of cicada wings. For example, these:
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3576530/
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3657783/
https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/decoding-the-chemistry-behind-cicadas-bacteria-killing-wings/4011805.article
and others. In that case, it would literally not matter what species or genus of cicada it was based on, all that would matter is recognizing that it's a cicada wing.
That gives perhaps a different possible direction, if the nature of the clue is an oblique reference to bacteria, or hydrophobicity, or nanopillars, etc. - or even the name or identity of one of the researchers working on this topic.

My apologies for not replying sooner.  I have had a look through parts a collection of cicadas from around the world, which I have recently had access to (though not extensively as I have not had much time), and I have not found a match worth noting. There are actually a fair number of cicadas that share features with that wing, different features for different genera, and I haven't seen any that match a lot more closely than many others. One of my colleagues was also unable to suggest a matching taxon.

All I can really say with confidence is that it is indeed probably a cicada (family Cicadidae).  And while the drawing has a sketchy quality, as if done quickly or casually, there is so much about it that is biologically accurate that I wouldn't be surprised if there is a species out there somewhere that was used as the model.  There is really only one cell that is close to being implausible in its shape, the one at the very bottom center of the wing.  But cicadas commonly develop weird abnormalities in wing venation that appear in the odd individual.

[4/12/2024 4:23 PM] acab_adabara
What's interesting is that 641 is the 116th prime, and 709 the 127th. So their indexes differ by 11, which just so happens to be the difference between the indexes of 107/167/229, which were used for the filenames of one of the koans in the solved pages.


[4/12/2024 4:24 PM] acab_adabara

[4/12/2024 4:29 PM] acab_adabara
